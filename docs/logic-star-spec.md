# Logic Star — Specification

Status: **v1.0 shipped.** All four modes are live: Logic Grid, Dragon Isle,
Number Patterns, and Number Boxes. Profiles, parent PIN, difficulty tiers,
progress tracking, and CSV export ship with it.
Target file: `logic-star.html` (self-contained, alongside Math Star, Spelling Star, and Geography Star)

---

## 1. Goals

Deduction practice for roughly ages 9–14, matching the existing Star Apps: a single
self-contained HTML file, offline-capable, localStorage progress, parent-managed
profiles, no network dependency at runtime.

Unlike the other Star apps there is no fact base to memorise. Every puzzle is
**generated on the device**, so the app never runs out of material and two children
never get the same round.

### Non-goals (v1)

- Hand-authored puzzle packs, or any puzzle content in the repo
- Puzzle types needing prose comprehension beyond a few sentences (riddles, word problems)
- Timed competition, leaderboards, or anything the child can lose

---

## 2. Content slant

Themes lean to what this family actually does rather than classroom scenes:
animals and dinosaurs, dragons, horses (two of the eleven themes), video games,
art, vacations, and co-op. The eleven grid themes are: Fossil Dig, Dragon
Sanctuary, Riding Lesson Day, Trail Ride, Game Night Tournament, Game Jam,
Studio Afternoon, Co-op Art Show, Road Trip, Co-op Workshop Day, and Aquarium
Volunteers. Names come from a shared twenty-name pool, so the same theme reads
differently each time.

## 3. Modes

| Mode | What it is | Round |
|---|---|---|
| Logic Grid | Classic grid-logic puzzle: a story, numbered clues, and a marking grid | 1 puzzle |
| Dragon Isle | Knights-and-knaves. Honest dragons always tell the truth, fibbers always lie | 4 puzzles |
| Number Patterns | Find the rule, give the next term | 8 questions |
| Number Boxes | Sudoku at 4×4, 6×6, or 9×9 | 1 puzzle |

## 4. Difficulty

A tier is set per child by the parent; the tier decides which **levels** the
child may choose from at the start of each round.

| Tier | Levels offered |
|---|---|
| Ages 9–10 | Warm-up, Steady |
| Ages 11–12 | Warm-up, Steady, Tricky |
| Ages 13–14 | Steady, Tricky, Brain-bender |
| Let them pick | all four |

What a level means per mode:

| Level | Logic Grid | Dragon Isle | Patterns | Sudoku |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | 3 people × 2 attributes, direct clues allowed | 2 dragons | add, subtract, skip count | 4×4, 8 given |
| Steady | 4 × 2, ordering clues, "either/or" clues | 3 dragons | + times tables, growing steps, alternating | 6×6, 16 given |
| Tricky | 4 × 3, **no** clue names a person and an attribute directly | 3 dragons, counting claims | + squares, Fibonacci, two-step rules | 6×6, 12 given |
| Brain-bender | 5 × 3, same restriction | 4 dragons | anything | 9×9, 30 given |

## 5. Puzzle correctness

This is the part that matters most: a child must never be asked to guess, and
never be told they are wrong when they are right.

**Logic Grid.** The generator picks a solution first, then builds a pool of
clues that are true of it, then adds clues one at a time until a
**constraint-propagation solver** can finish the puzzle. That solver only uses
moves a child can make on paper — a row or column with one survivor is an
assignment, a ✓ clears the rest of its row and column, and cross-elimination
through a middle category ("if Ava can't be with any dragon that has emerald
scales, Ava isn't emerald"). Ordering clues get their own propagators. Because
propagation alone finishes the puzzle, **no guessing is ever required**.

Clues are then removed one at a time for as long as propagation still solves the
puzzle, which keeps clue lists short (3–5 at Warm-up, 8–13 at Brain-bender).
Finally a brute-force count over all assignments confirms exactly one solution —
a cheap belt-and-braces check against a bug in the propagators.

**Dragon Isle.** Types are assigned first, then each dragon gets a statement
whose truth value matches its type. A brute-force pass over all 2ⁿ labellings
confirms exactly one is consistent; puzzles with 0 or 2+ solutions are discarded
and regenerated.

**Sudoku.** A filled grid is generated by backtracking, then cells are removed
one at a time, each removal kept only if a solution counter still finds exactly
one solution.

**Patterns.** Generated from a known rule, and the rule is shown with the answer
so a near-miss becomes a teaching moment rather than a dead end.

## 6. Help, not penalties

- **Hint** on the grid reveals one true pairing, marks it gold, and locks it.
  Hints are counted in the round record but never subtracted from anything.
- **Check my grid** distinguishes three states: a wrong ✓ (shown in red, with a
  nudge to re-read the clues), an incomplete grid (says how many rows still need
  a ✓), and a finished grid.
- **Show me the answer** always exists. Taking it records the round as unsolved
  and shows the full solution table.
- Auto-✗ (placing a ✓ clears the rest of its row and column) is on by default
  and can be switched off per child.
- The running timer can be switched off per child.

---

## 7. Storage and privacy

Same shape as the other Star apps: one localStorage record per child under the
`logicstar-` prefix, holding name, parent PIN, tier, settings, per-mode/per-level
counts, and the most recent 500 rounds. Nothing leaves the device. Damaged
records are surfaced on the profile picker rather than silently dropped, and
the parent dashboard offers reset and delete, both PIN-confirmed.

CSV export writes one row per round: child, date, time, mode, level, result,
score, out of, hints, seconds.

---

## 8. Testing

The generators were developed and exercised outside the browser first: 60
puzzles per level per mode, asserting a single solution, no-guess solvability,
and clue text free of template artefacts. The shipped app was then driven
end-to-end in Chromium — setup wizard, all four modes to their end screens,
parent dashboard, reload persistence, and a 390px-wide phone viewport — checking
for console errors and for horizontal page overflow.
